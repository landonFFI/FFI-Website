It's 11 p.m. A guest walks up to your front desk. "Is there an ATM around here?"

Your night clerk points down the road to a gas station. The guest sighs, gets back in the car, and drives off to find cash in a town they don't know.

If that happens once a year, you don't need an ATM. If your front desk hears it every week, you're sending guests away from your property, at night, to solve a problem you could solve in the lobby.

Here's how to tell which kind of property you run.

Do hotel guests still need cash?

Fewer than before, but plenty still do.

The Federal Reserve's 2026 study of how Americans pay found that more than 90% of consumers have no plans to stop using cash, and more than three out of four still carry it. Cash shows up most for small, fast payments. That's exactly what a hotel guest runs into:

  • Tips. Housekeeping, the bell desk, valet, and the shuttle driver.
  • Vending and snacks, if your machines are older or cash-only.
  • Parking, tolls, and cabs in some places.
  • Cash-only spots nearby, like a local barbecue place, a flea market, or a food truck.
  • Event weekends, when guests are headed to a game, a tailgate, or a festival where cash moves faster.

Tips are the big one. In Bankrate's 2025 tipping survey, 25% of Americans said they always tip hotel housekeepers. A 2023 Canary Technologies survey of hotel guests found that 79% think hotel workers should get tips. A guest who wants to leave $5 on the pillow needs a $5 bill.

For more on where cash stands, see our post on whether cash is really dying.

What does an ATM actually do for a hotel?

Four things, starting with the one that matters most.

It answers the front desk question. Your staff stop giving directions to a gas station. Guests get what they need without leaving.

It keeps guests safe and on the property. A traveler walking or driving to an unknown ATM late at night is not the experience you want them to remember. A lobby machine in view of your desk and cameras is a better answer.

It helps your staff get tipped. Housekeepers and bell staff are often the ones who lose out when guests have no cash. An ATM in the lobby makes it easier for a guest who wants to tip to actually do it.

It earns you a share of the fees. Every withdrawal carries a surcharge, and with free placement you get a share of it. For scale, Bankrate's 2025 study found the average surcharge at bank-owned ATMs hit a record $3.22. We'd treat this as the smallest reason, not the biggest.

Here's a simple test. For two weeks, have your front desk keep a tally every time a guest asks where to get cash. If the count is under a handful, skip it. If it's more than that, you have your answer.

What are the downsides for a hotel?

Let's be straight about these.

  • It takes some lobby space. About the size of a small vending machine.
  • Some guests dislike the fee. A guest who uses a fee-charging ATM is choosing to pay for the convenience. Clear placement, not a machine in their face at check-in, keeps it from feeling pushy.
  • It may not get used much at a card-heavy property. That's why the two-week tally matters.
  • A bad ATM company is worse than none. A lobby machine that's always empty or "out of service" makes your property look run-down. Read what an out-of-order ATM really costs before you sign with anyone.

Where should the ATM go in a hotel?

In the lobby, where guests can find it and your staff can see it.

  • In view of the front desk and a camera. Good for security and for guests.
  • Near the vending area or the hallway to the elevators. Easy to reach, but not the first thing guests see at check-in.
  • Out of the main walkway and away from exits. Fire code and foot traffic come first.
  • With clear space in front, so a guest in a wheelchair can use it.
  • Near a standard outlet. That's the only hookup it needs. Our machines connect by wireless cellular service, so there's no phone line and nothing on your guest Wi-Fi or hotel network.

The bottom line

A lobby ATM isn't right for every hotel. It earns its spot when guests ask your front desk for cash, when they arrive late or head to events, and when there's no ATM close by. It's a weak fit for a card-heavy property next to a bank.

Count the cash questions at your desk for two weeks. If your staff keep pointing guests down the road, it's time to keep those guests in your lobby instead.
