Can You Charge Customers for Using a Credit Card in Alabama?

Alabama just changed how credit card fees are taxed. Here's whether surcharging is legal in Alabama, and how it's different from a cash discount or dual pricing.

By Landon Ford, owner of Ford Frontier Investments, Birmingham, Alabama.
Updated September 24, 2026. About a 5-minute read.

Can You Charge Customers for Using a Credit Card in Alabama?

The short answer

Yes. Alabama has no state law against surcharging, so credit card surcharges are legal here as long as you follow Visa and Mastercard's rules: cap it at your real cost (network rules put a ceiling around 4%), never surcharge a debit card, and disclose it clearly. As of September 1, 2026, a new Alabama law also excludes a properly disclosed card fee from sales tax.

You watch every card swipe cost you money. A customer pays with a credit card, and a few cents to a few dollars of that sale go straight to the processor, not your register. Do that a few hundred times a month, and it adds up fast.

You've probably wondered if you can pass that cost back to the customer. Maybe a competitor already does it, with a little sign by the register. Maybe someone told you it's illegal. Maybe you tried to look it up and got three different answers.

Here's the honest, current answer for Alabama, plus what changed on September 1, 2026.

This is general information, not legal advice. Card network rules and state law both change, so check with your processor or an attorney before you turn on a new fee.

Can you legally charge customers extra for using a credit card in Alabama?

Yes. Alabama has no state law that bans or limits credit card surcharges. Some states do restrict it. Alabama isn't one of them.

That doesn't mean anything goes. Visa and Mastercard, not the state, write the rules you actually have to follow:

  • You have to tell your processor and register your intent to surcharge before you start.
  • The fee can't be more than what the card actually costs you to accept.
  • The fee has to be shown clearly, at the door and again on the receipt, before the customer pays.
  • You can never surcharge a debit card, even one run as credit.

Break those rules and the risk isn't a state fine. It's your merchant account. Visa and Mastercard actively watch for surcharge violations, and losing your ability to accept cards is a much bigger problem than the fee itself.

What's the difference between a surcharge, a cash discount, and dual pricing?

People use these terms like they mean the same thing. They don't, and the difference matters for what you're required to post and disclose.

How it works What the customer sees
Surcharge You add a fee only on credit card payments One price, plus a card fee at checkout
Cash discount The posted price is the card price; cash customers get money off One posted price, with a discount for paying cash
Dual pricing You show two prices everywhere, side by side A cash price and a card price, listed together

Debit and prepaid cards are never surcharged, under any of these programs. That rule comes from the card networks and applies in all 50 states, not just Alabama.

Dual pricing is generally the easiest one to stay compliant with, because nothing is added or taken away at the register. The two prices are just posted, and the customer picks. Cash discounts and surcharges both work, but they come with stricter signage rules: the discount or fee has to be posted where the customer sees it before they decide how to pay, not sprung on them at the counter.

What changed with Alabama's new sales tax law?

Before September 1, 2026, if you charged a customer a separate credit card fee in Alabama, the state made you charge sales tax on that fee too, on top of the sales tax on the purchase itself.

That changed under Senate Bill 221, sponsored by Senator Arthur Orr and signed into law by Governor Kay Ivey. As of September 1, 2026, a credit card transaction fee is excluded from Alabama sales and use tax, as long as it's separate and identifiable on the receipt.

The Alabama Department of Revenue is direct about the catch: if your receipt shows one lump total that includes the fee, the whole amount is still taxed. The exemption only applies when the fee is broken out as its own line.

What rules do you actually have to follow?

Put together, here's what a compliant program looks like in Alabama today:

  1. Register with your processor first. Visa and Mastercard both require advance notice before you start surcharging.
  2. Cap the fee at your real cost. Card network rules put a ceiling around 4%, but the fee still can't exceed what the card actually costs you to process.
  3. Never touch debit or prepaid. That line doesn't move, in Alabama or anywhere else.
  4. Post it before the sale. Signage at the entrance and at the point of sale, not just a note on the receipt after the fact.
  5. Break the fee out on the receipt. Beyond being a network rule, this is also what keeps the fee out of your Alabama sales tax base under the new law.

If a business ATM sits by your register, the same math applies from the other direction: cash customers who use it skip the card fee question completely, which is one reason a bar with cash on hand sees fewer card-fee complaints than one without.

Should your business actually do this?

That depends on your card mix and your margins. A gas station or convenience store running mostly small-ticket sales feels every basis point of card cost. A business with mostly large tickets or mostly cash customers may not gain much from adding a program on top of what they're already paying to process cards.

The bigger risk we see isn't the legality question. It's businesses that set this up themselves, get the signage or the percentage wrong, and find out during a card network audit instead of before one. Getting your current processing statement reviewed before you flip the switch is the cheap way to find that out.

The bottom line

Credit card surcharging is legal in Alabama. So are cash discount and dual pricing programs. The rules that matter come from Visa and Mastercard, not the state: cap the fee at your real cost, never touch debit, and disclose it clearly. As of September 1, 2026, Alabama also stopped taxing a properly disclosed card fee, which makes doing this correctly worth a little more than it used to be.

Get it set up right the first time, and it's one more line item off your books. Get it wrong, and it's a call from your processor you don't want.
