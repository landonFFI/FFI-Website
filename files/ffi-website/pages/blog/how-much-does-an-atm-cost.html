How much cash do you need to fill an ATM?

This is the cost most people forget. And it's often bigger than the machine.

An ATM only works if it has cash in it. You load your own money into the machine. When customers withdraw, the machine empties. You refill it.

Here's a simple way to estimate how much you need:

Weekly withdrawals × average withdrawal amount = cash needed each week

Say your machine does 40 withdrawals a week, and the average withdrawal is about $70. That's roughly $2,800 going out each week. You need at least that much on hand between refills, plus a cushion so the machine never runs dry on a busy weekend.

For a sense of scale, a 2024 survey of in-store ATM users by NCR Atleos found an average withdrawal of $68.

The good news: this cash isn't spent. It's still your money. When a customer takes out $60, their bank sends $60 back through the payment network. With our processing, that money usually lands in your account within 24 hours on business days, Monday through Friday. Weekends and bank holidays take longer.

So the cash is a float, not a cost. But you have to have it. If tying up a few thousand dollars would squeeze your business, that's a real sign to look at free ATM placement instead, where we supply all the cash.

What does it cost to set up an ATM?

Setup has four parts. Here's what each one costs when you buy from us.

Shipping: free in the continental US. Every machine ships free to addresses in the continental United States.

Processing setup: free with FFI processing. Processing connects your ATM to the banking networks so it can hand out cash. When you process with us, through our partners PAI and DNS, setup costs nothing. If you'd rather use an outside processor, setup is $500.

Programming: free sheet, or paid factory programming. Every machine comes with a free ATM programming sheet, sent to your email with the paperwork you need to get set up for processing. It walks you through programming step by step. Our team answers questions before, during, and after setup. If you'd rather have the machine programmed before it ships, factory programming is $150.

Installation: not included, but we can help. Many owners install their own machine. If you want someone to do it, we can send an on-site technician from our network of operators and technicians for $250.

Timing: we place your order within 12 hours of receiving payment. Delivery usually takes about two weeks, depending on your location. When delays happen, they come from the manufacturer.

What does an ATM cost to run each month?

After setup, expect these ongoing costs:

  • Connection. Your ATM needs a way to talk to the network. Our machines use wireless cellular service, billed monthly per ATM. See wireless ATM service.
  • Receipt paper and supplies. Small, but steady.
  • Cash loading. Free if you do it yourself. Your time isn't free, though. If you'd rather not, we offer cash loading for $1 per transaction.
  • Repairs after the warranty. Parts wear out. Budget for it.
  • Insurance. Ask your insurance agent how an ATM and the cash inside it fit your policy.

Zero dollars.

You don't keep 100% of the fees, the way an owner does. But you also don't spend thousands up front, tie up your cash, or take on another job.

So is buying an ATM worth the cost?

It can be. Buying makes sense when three things are true:

  1. Your location is busy enough. More transactions means the machine pays for itself faster.
  2. You have the cash to keep it full. Without cash, the machine earns nothing.
  3. You have the time, or you'll pay for loading. Someone has to show up before it runs dry.

If all three are true, owning lets you keep every dollar of the surcharge. If any one of them isn't true, free placement will probably put more money in your pocket with less risk.
