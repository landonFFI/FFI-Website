It's a Friday night and the bar is three deep.

A customer squeezes up, orders a round, and pulls out his wallet. Nothing but cards. Maybe your card reader is backed up. Maybe it's a cash-only special. Maybe he just wants to tip your bartender in cash. So he says, "I'll be right back. Going to find an ATM."

Some of those customers do come back. Some of them walk two blocks, find cash at another bar, run into a friend, and spend the rest of the night there.

That's the real question behind "does my bar need an ATM?" It's not about the ATM. It's about how many customers you're sending out the door.

## Do people still use cash at bars?

Yes. Less than they used to, but a lot more than most people assume.

The Federal Reserve's 2026 study of how Americans pay found that more than 90% of consumers have no plans to stop using cash. More than three out of four still carry it. Cash has been the third most-used way to pay for six years running, behind only debit and credit cards.

Bars are one of the places cash sticks around the longest:

- **Tips.** Many customers still tip in cash, and many bartenders prefer it.
- **Cover charges.** A cash door moves faster than a card reader at 11 p.m.
- **Fast rounds.** Cash is quicker than opening and closing a tab.
- **Splitting.** Groups split rounds with cash.
- **Budgeting.** Some people bring cash on purpose so they don't overspend.

Every one of those moments ends the same way when a customer is short: they need an ATM. The only question is whether it's yours.

## What happens when your bar doesn't have an ATM?

Your customers still need cash. They just go get it somewhere else.

And "somewhere else" is getting farther away. Bank branches keep closing, including in Birmingham's nightlife areas. According to the Alabama State Banking Department, a bank branch in Five Points South closed in October 2025. When the bank on the corner closes, the nearest ATM might be a gas station down the road, or another bar.

Every trip out the door is a chance for that customer to change their plans for the night.

## What does an ATM actually do for a bar?

Three things.

**It keeps customers inside.** This is the big one. A customer who can get cash at your bar spends it at your bar. In a 2024 survey of in-store ATM users by NCR Atleos, 55% of people who came to a store specifically to use its ATM made a purchase while they were there. (That survey looked at fee-free machines, so bar numbers will differ. But the pattern holds: cash access keeps people spending where they are.)

**It earns you a share of every surcharge.** With free placement, each withdrawal comes with a small fee paid by the customer. Part of that fee comes to you, deposited at the start of each month. Bars and nightclubs tend to do more ATM transactions per customer than regular stores, because so much of the night runs on cash. See how to estimate yours in [how much a business can make from a free ATM](how-much-does-a-business-make-from-an-atm.html).

**It can cut some card fees.** Every card swipe costs you a processing fee. When a customer pulls cash from your ATM and pays with it, that sale doesn't carry a card fee.

## Where should an ATM go in a bar?

Placement matters. A machine in the wrong spot does half the business of one in the right spot. Here's what works:

- **Where customers see it right away.** Near the entrance or along the path to the restrooms.
- **Where your staff can see it.** It should never be in a dark corner nobody watches.
- **In camera view.** Good for security, and good for settling any "the machine didn't give me my money" claims.
- **Out of the way of exits and walkways.** Fire code and crowd flow come first.
- **With clear space in front.** So customers in wheelchairs can reach it.
- **Near a standard outlet.** That's the only hookup it needs. Our machines connect by wireless cellular service, so no phone line or internet.

When we visit your bar, we'll walk the space with you and recommend the best spot.
