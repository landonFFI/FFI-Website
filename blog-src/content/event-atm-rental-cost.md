"How much does it cost to get an ATM at my event?"

It's the first question every organizer asks. It's also one that no honest ATM company can answer in one number without asking you a few questions first. A one-day church fundraiser and a three-weekend festival are completely different jobs.

Here's what actually sets the price, why events cost more than a store ATM, and how to make sure your quote has no surprises.

## What sets the price of an event ATM?

These are the six factors we look at for every event.

### 1. Distance

How far is your event from the operator? Machines, cash, and people all have to travel there and back, sometimes more than once.

### 2. How many days it runs

A one-day event is one setup and one takedown. A three-weekend event means more days of cash, security, and staffing.

### 3. Expected attendance

More people usually means more withdrawals, more cash, and sometimes more machines. Honest numbers matter. Guessing high or low both cost you.

### 4. How cash-heavy the event is

This one moves the price more than most organizers expect. We ask:

- Is there a **cash-only entry** or parking fee?
- Is there a **cash-only bar**?
- Are there **cash-only vendors**?
- Is there **no card acceptance** at all?

Every "yes" means guests will need more cash, which means more cash has to be on-site and ready.

### 5. How many machines you need

Bigger or more spread-out events may need more than one machine. See [how many ATMs your event needs](festival-event-atm-planning-alabama.html).

### 6. Site needs

Is there power where the machines will go, or will you need a generator? Where can the machines be placed safely? How early can we set up?

## Why do event ATMs cost more than a store ATM?

Because an event is a much bigger job for the ATM company.

**It takes a lot more cash.** A store ATM might go through its cash over days or weeks. An event machine can go through the same amount in hours. One county fair in Washington state reported its six ATMs dispensed nearly $200,000 over a single opening weekend. All of that cash has to be on hand, on-site, at the right time.

**It needs on-site security.** A permanent store ATM is installed in place. An event machine isn't bolted down the way a long-term machine is. That means security has to be there for the machines and the cash.

**It takes travel, setup, and takedown.** Every event is a round trip, plus setup before your gates open and takedown after they close.

**The risk is higher.** If attendance comes in far below what was planned, the ATM company has still brought the cash, the machines, and the people. One event ATM operator told ATM Marketplace about a concert where the promoter's estimate fell from 100,000 to 150,000 people down to 25,000 to 50,000 by the time he arrived.

That's why events are priced higher than a machine that sits in one location long-term.

## Who pays for an event ATM?

Two groups pay in different ways.

**Your guests** pay a surcharge each time they withdraw cash, just like at any ATM.

**You, the organizer,** may or may not pay depending on your event. Depending on the factors above, event pricing can include a setup fee, a share of the surcharge revenue, or a mix of both. Very cash-heavy events with big crowds can look very different from small, short events far away.

Whatever the structure, get it in writing before the event: what you pay, what you earn, when you're paid, and what's included.

## What should your event ATM quote include?

A clear quote answers these questions:

- How many machines, and where will they go?
- How much cash will be on-site, and how will machines stay full during peak hours?
- What security comes with the machines?
- When is setup and takedown?
- What do you need to provide, like power, space, and access?
- What do you pay, what do you earn, and when?
- What happens if a machine has a problem during the event?

If a quote doesn't cover these, ask before you sign.

## How do you get an accurate quote?

Send your ATM provider:

1. Event name, dates, and hours
2. The address or venue
3. Expected attendance per day (be honest)
4. Cash-only entry, bar, or vendors, and whether cards are accepted at all
5. Whether there's power where machines would go
6. Anything unusual, like a remote site or late-night hours
