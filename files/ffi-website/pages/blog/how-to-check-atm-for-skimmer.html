Buying and Owning an ATM

How Do I Check My Store's ATM for a Card Skimmer?

A skimmer takes about a minute to check for. Here's what to look for, how often, and what to do the moment you find something.

By Landon Ford, owner of Ford Frontier Investments, Birmingham, Alabama.
Updated October 15, 2026. About a 4-minute read.

How Do I Check My Store's ATM for a Card Skimmer?

The short answer

Look at the card slot and keypad every day. Know how the machine normally looks, then check for a slot that sticks out, a loose part, glue or tape, a color that doesn't match, or a keypad that feels raised. Gently tug the card reader. If anything is off, stop using the machine, don't touch it more than needed, and call your ATM provider and the police.

Your customers trust the ATM in your store. If a thief hides a skimmer on it, they may blame you, even though you did nothing wrong.

It happens more than most owners think. In January 2026, the U.S. Secret Service said its teams visited more than 9,000 businesses during 2025. They inspected nearly 60,000 payment terminals, gas pumps, and ATMs, and pulled 411 illegal skimming devices. In February 2026, the agency warned that criminals are still putting them on ATMs and gas pumps.

The good news is that a check takes about a minute. Here's how to do it.

What is an ATM skimmer?

A skimmer is a device that copies the data from a customer's card. Thieves often pair it with a tiny camera or a fake keypad to steal the PIN too. The goal is to make copies of the card and drain the account.

It targets your customers, not your cash. It still hurts your store, because people remember where it happened. For more on theft and who pays when a machine is stolen, see our guide to ATM security.

What does a skimmer look like?

Most skimmers sit on top of the real card slot. They are made to blend in, but they rarely match perfectly. Look for:

  • A card slot that sticks out, looks thicker, or sits crooked
  • A color, texture, or finish that doesn't match the panel around it
  • Glue, tape, or sticky residue near the slot
  • Scratches or gaps around the reader
  • A keypad that feels raised, soft, or bulky
  • Tiny holes or odd bits of plastic near the keypad, which can hide a camera

Guides from consumer and trade groups all say the same thing. Know what the machine looks like normally. A small change is the clue.

How do I check my ATM for a skimmer?

Do this at open and again at close. It takes less than a minute.

The daily check

  1. Look at the card slot. Does it look the same as yesterday?
  2. Gently tug the card reader. It should not move or come off.
  3. Press the keypad with your hand. Keys should feel the same as always.
  4. Look above and beside the keypad for a pinhole or an extra piece.
  5. Check the area around the machine for anything new, like a brochure holder that now points at the keypad.

Take one clear photo of the machine when it's clean. Then you have something to compare against.

A tug test won't catch every device. Some skimmers are hidden inside the machine or are very well made. That's why the next steps matter too.

What should I do if I find a skimmer?

Stay calm and move in this order:

  1. Stop customers from using it. Put an "out of order" sign on it or block it.
  2. Don't handle it more than you need to. Police may want it left as found.
  3. Call the police. Ask for a report number.
  4. Call your ATM provider. They can reach the bank and the processor and get the machine checked.
  5. Save your camera footage. It may show who installed the device and when.
  6. Tell your staff. They should know what happened and who to call.

Some states add legal duties. Texas, for example, now requires businesses that find a skimmer to tell police and disable the terminal. Alabama's rules may differ, so ask the police and your provider what applies to you. This is general information, not legal advice.

Can I make skimming harder in the first place?

You can't make it impossible, but you can make it harder.

  • Put the machine where staff can see it. Thieves want a place where nobody watches. A machine near the register, in a lit spot, is a worse target.
  • Add cameras that cover the machine. They also help if you ever need footage.
  • Teach your staff the daily check. Everyone who opens or closes should know it.
  • Ask about tap-to-pay. When a customer taps instead of inserting a card, the card never enters the slot. Tap-to-pay is available for qualifying locations from Ford Frontier Investments.
  • Remind customers to cover the keypad. It blocks hidden cameras.

Who is responsible if a customer is skimmed at my ATM?

That depends on your agreement and the facts. Many owners never ask until something goes wrong.

When you place an ATM with a company, you should be able to see in writing who handles inspections, who you call, and who is responsible if something goes wrong. If your current agreement is silent on this, that is worth fixing. Our guide to placement agreement red flags shows what a clear one covers.

If a customer says they were charged and didn't get cash, that is a different problem. See what to do when a customer says your ATM didn't give them their money.

The bottom line

A skimmer check is a one-minute habit. Look at the slot and keypad every day, tug the reader, and know who to call if something is off.

If you're not sure who is responsible for the ATM in your store, or what your provider checks, ask. A good ATM company will tell you straight.
