Is Your ATM Affected by the Windows 10 End of Support in October 2026?

Microsoft stops patching the Windows 10 version used in many ATMs on October 13, 2026. Here's what that means for your store, and the questions to ask this week.

By Landon Ford, owner of Ford Frontier Investments, Birmingham, Alabama.
Updated October 8, 2026. About a 5-minute read.

Is Your ATM Affected by the Windows 10 End of Support in October 2026?

The short answer

Microsoft ends support for Windows 10 LTSB 2016, the version many US ATMs use, on October 13, 2026. After that date it stops issuing security patches. Your ATM will not shut off, but it becomes a bigger security and compliance risk. Ask whoever runs your ATM which Windows version it uses and what the upgrade plan is.

Most people never think about what runs inside an ATM. It is a computer in a safe, and it runs Windows like an office PC does.

That matters this week. On October 13, 2026, Microsoft ends support for the Windows 10 version that is most common in ATMs across the country.

It won't shut your ATM off. But it can turn an old machine into a security and compliance risk, and the people who get surprised are the ones who never asked.

Here's what is happening, how to find out if it touches you, and what to ask.

What is ending on October 13, 2026?

Microsoft sells Windows in "long-term" editions meant for machines like ATMs, kiosks, and registers. The 2016 edition, called Windows 10 LTSB 2016, reaches end of support on October 13, 2026, according to Windows Central and Neowin.

On that date, devices get a final monthly security update. After that, Microsoft stops sending security patches, bug fixes, and technical support for that edition.

Hyosung Americas has said this 2016 edition is the one most US ATMs use, because it had the longest support life of the Windows 10 versions.

Not every Windows 10 ATM is hit. Later builds last longer. One industry guide lists the 2019 build running to January 2029 and the 2021 build to January 2032. Plain "Windows 10" for home computers already ended in October 2025, and that is a different thing.

Does my ATM run Windows 10?

Maybe, maybe not. Many machines, especially older ones, run other software. A newer machine could be on a later build.

You can't tell from the screen. So ask. The three things to get are:

  1. The ATM's make, model, and age.
  2. The Windows version and edition it runs.
  3. Who manages updates for it: you, your ATM company, or your processor.

If you own the machine, the manufacturer can usually answer from the model and serial number. If an ATM company placed it in your store, the question belongs to them. A good company answers it in one email.

What happens to an ATM after support ends?

The machine keeps taking cards and paying out cash. Nothing breaks on day one.

What changes is that new security holes found after October 13 will not get a fix from Microsoft. Trade sources, like Cennox, point out that running an unsupported system leaves a machine open to malware and can bring fees for falling out of line with card security standards.

For a store, the real worry is not a movie-style hack. It is the slow kind: a machine that can't keep up with the rules, gets flagged, and goes down at the worst time. We covered what downtime costs in what an "out of order" ATM really costs your business.

Can an older ATM be upgraded?

Sometimes. This is where it gets uneven.

  • Some machines just need a new computer core. The ATM body is fine, and a new brain makes it run a supported Windows version.
  • Some machines can't be upgraded. The hardware is too old, or the maker never certified the upgrade.
  • Paid extended support exists as a bridge. Windows Central reports Microsoft offers extended security updates for up to three years. For the ATM edition, they come through the device maker. The price goes up each year.
  • Windows 11 is coming. Manufacturers say they are working toward it, but timing depends on the model.

Cennox notes that ATM upgrades often involve long planning because of hardware and certification. Anyone who leaves this to the last week usually pays for it.

Who pays for the upgrade?

That depends on who owns the machine, and this is where owners get burned.

  • You own the ATM. The upgrade or replacement is probably your cost. Get a quote from the manufacturer for your exact model before you decide.
  • An ATM company placed the machine. The machine is theirs, so the upgrade question should be theirs too. Ask them in writing what their plan is. Check your agreement for who handles compliance, as we explain in what's normal and what's a red flag in an ATM agreement.
  • You bought it used. Be careful. Older used machines are the most likely to be stuck on an old edition. See buying a used ATM online for what to check.

What should you ask your ATM company this week?

Keep it short. Send these three questions by email so the answers are in writing:

  1. What Windows edition does my ATM run?
  2. Is it affected by the October 13, 2026 end of support?
  3. If so, what is the plan, the date, and who pays?

If you get a vague answer, or no answer, that tells you something. It is the same signal we describe in 12 questions to ask any ATM company.

What if the machine is too old to keep?

Then the honest move is to replace it, not to patch it for another year. If you host an old machine that already has problems, you may qualify for a new one. The bottom line

October 13 won't switch off your ATM. It is a deadline for whoever is responsible for keeping it secure. Find out who that is, ask three questions, and get the answers in writing.
