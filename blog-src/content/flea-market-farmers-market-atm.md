It's a good Saturday. The lot is full, the aisles are packed, and shoppers are holding up a pretty good find.

Then the vendor says, "Cash only." The shopper pats their pockets, sighs, and walks off to look for a machine. Some come back. A lot of them don't.

If you run a flea market, trade day, or farmers market, you see this every week. Holiday markets across Alabama are coming up this fall, and cash will be on shoppers' minds. Here's how an ATM can help, and what to check first.

## Do flea markets and farmers markets still run on cash?

Yes. Many booth owners don't take cards. Card readers are cheap now, but they need a signal, a charged phone, and a vendor who wants to bother with fees.

Cash is still common for shoppers, too. The Federal Reserve's 2026 study found more than 90% of consumers have no plans to stop using cash.

Some markets do fine without an ATM. One Texas farmers market group says on its site that it has none, and points shoppers to cash, cards at some booths, and SNAP and WIC benefits. If almost every vendor takes cards, you may not need one.

But if even a handful of your booths are cash only, those booths lose sales every time a shopper has to go find cash.

## What does an ATM do for a market?

**It keeps shoppers on your grounds.** A shopper who gets cash at the front gate spends it inside. One who leaves to find a machine may not return.

**It helps your vendors sell.** Your vendors are your customers, too. When shoppers can get cash, the cash-only booths make more sales, and they stay happy with your market.

**It can earn the market money.** The host usually gets a share of each surcharge. A North Carolina farmers market reported in 2011 that its ATM fee went back to the market instead of a bank, and it planned to use that money for events.

**It may bring more spending.** A Florida flea market owner told ATM Marketplace in 2013 that about half the cash taken from his two machines came back to his merchants as sales. That was one owner's estimate, and it's more than a decade old. Treat it as a hint, not a promise.

## What are the downsides?

Be honest with yourself about these before you say yes.

- **Shoppers pay a fee.** Anyone using a machine from outside their bank pays a surcharge. Put the machine where the fee is clear on the screen, and tell shoppers up front.
- **Cash and equipment can be targets.** Open-air markets are busy, but they're quiet when closed.
- **Someone has to keep it full.** A machine that's empty at noon on a Saturday is worse than no machine.
- **Power outdoors.** A machine needs a safe outlet. A generator alone may not be enough if the machine can't connect.

All four have fixes. They're also the reasons to choose your ATM company with care.

## Where should the ATM go at a market?

- **Near the entrance,** so shoppers get cash before they start buying.
- **Where cash-only booths cluster,** such as food, crafts, and produce.
- **In a lit, visible spot** with staff or foot traffic nearby.
- **Under cover and near power.** Sun, rain, and dust are hard on equipment.

If your market sprawls across a large lot, two machines in two places can work better than two side by side. For more on spacing machines, see [how many ATMs an event needs](festival-event-atm-planning-alabama.html).

## Who pays for and runs the machine?

This is where markets get stuck. You don't want a second job.

When the City of Muskegon, Michigan asked for an ATM provider for its farmers market in 2024, it spelled out the job. The provider had to fill the machine with cash, service it, and carry $1,000,000 in insurance that covered the machine and its contents. The shopper paid the surcharge, and the city paid nothing. ## What should you ask before you say yes?

- Who owns the machine and the cash?
- Who loads it, and what happens when it runs out on a busy day?
- Who do I call when it breaks, and will a real person answer?
- How and when does the market get paid?
- What's in the agreement, and who pays if the machine is stolen?

If your vendors take cash and your shoppers can't get any, you're losing sales you already earned. An ATM fixes that, and it doesn't have to cost the market a dime or add to your workload.

Pick a provider who owns the machine, loads the cash, and answers the phone. Then put it where shoppers walk in.
