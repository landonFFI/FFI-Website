You've seen the headlines. Stores might soon turn away rewards cards. Swipe fees might drop. A judge in New York is deciding a case that's been going for about twenty years.

Then you look at your register and wonder if you're supposed to do something.

Here's the short version. Nothing has changed yet. But the deal had its final court hearing three days ago, and if it goes through, it gives you choices you've never had. It's worth knowing what they are before a sales rep calls you about them.

*This is general information, not legal advice. The deal is not final, and its terms could change.*

## What is the Visa and Mastercard settlement?

For two decades, merchants have sued Visa and Mastercard over swipe fees and the rules that come with them. Payments Dive reports the case started more than twenty years ago.

In November 2025, the two networks and the merchants' lawyers signed a new deal. It doesn't pay cash. It changes rules and rates going forward.

That matters, because a lot of owners mix it up with the older cash settlement. That one paid merchants who took cards from 2004 to 2019. Its claim deadline, February 4, 2025, has passed.

## Where does the settlement stand right now?

Here's the timeline so far:

| Date | What happened |
|---|---|
| November 2025 | Visa, Mastercard, and the merchants' lawyers sign the new deal |
| June 9, 2026 | Judge Brian Cogan gives it early approval |
| November 16, 2026 | Final hearing in Brooklyn; no ruling |
| Next | A ruling, with no date set; appeals expected |

Tech Times reported the day after the hearing that the judge did not rule and gave no date. Payments Dive reported he warned that saying no could lead to a long trial.

Not everyone wants the deal. About 1,000 merchants and trade groups objected, led by Walmart, the National Retail Federation, and the National Association of Convenience Stores. NACS says the deal brings no real change to how card fees are set. It has said it will appeal if the deal is approved.

So even a yes from the judge is not the end. Plan on months.

## What would change for your business if it's approved?

If the judge approves it, Visa and Mastercard would have 90 days to rewrite their rules. After that, four things would change.

### 1. You could refuse whole groups of cards

Today, the "honor all cards" rule says if you take Visa credit, you take every Visa credit card. The deal splits credit cards into three groups: standard consumer, premium consumer (most rewards cards), and commercial.

You could decline a whole group. You could not decline one bank's cards.

### 2. Credit swipe fees would drop a little

Credit card fees would be cut by 10 basis points, which is 0.10%, for five years. Standard consumer cards would be capped at 1.25% for eight years. Premium and commercial cards would not get that cap.

### 3. The surcharge cap would be 3%

You could add a fee of up to 3% on credit cards, or your real cost if that is lower. Alabama already allows surcharges, as we explain in [the rules for credit card fees in Alabama](alabama-credit-card-surcharge-law.html).

### 4. You could treat card groups differently

The point of the groups is choice. You might take standard cards with no fee and add a surcharge on premium ones, if the final rules allow it.

## Should you stop taking rewards cards?

Probably not, at least not without doing the math first.

Here is why. Premium cards do cost more to take. Moneywise reported a premium Visa Infinite card can cost a merchant 0.15% more than a Visa Signature card. On $10,000 a month in premium card sales, that's about $15 a month.

Now compare that to one lost sale. Say a regular spends $40 a week at your store and carries one rewards card. If a "we don't take that card" sign sends them down the road, you lose about $2,000 a year from one person. Commentators quoted in the coverage warn that refusing rewards cards could cost more in lost sales than it saves.

The honest answer depends on your numbers:

- **How many of your card sales are premium cards?** Your processing statement can show this.
- **What are your margins?** A store running thin margins on small tickets feels every point more than a shop with big tickets.
- **Where else can your customer go?** If the place across the street takes every card, refusing one is risky.

## What should you do now?

You don't need to change anything this week. But you can get ready:

1. **Find your card mix.** Know what share of your sales are premium, standard, and business cards.
2. **Know your effective rate.** Total fees divided by total card sales.
3. **Ignore pressure.** If a processor rep says you must switch now because of the settlement, that's a sales pitch. Nothing takes effect until the court rules and the networks rewrite their rules.
4. **Watch for the ruling.** Check the news or the official settlement website for the judge's decision.

And remember the oldest way around card fees: cash. A customer who pays cash costs you nothing to process. If your store has a working ATM by the counter, cash customers stay in your store instead of leaving to find one. That's one reason [an ATM pays off for gas stations](gas-station-atm.html) in more ways than the fee share.

The Visa and Mastercard settlement could let you refuse premium rewards cards, cut your credit fees a little, and set a 3% surcharge cap. But it isn't final. The judge hasn't ruled, and appeals are likely. Use the wait to learn your own numbers, so when the new rules arrive, you decide based on your store, not a headline.
