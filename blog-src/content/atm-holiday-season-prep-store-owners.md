It's the Saturday before Christmas. Your store is full. Someone walks to the ATM, and the screen says "Out of Service."

They don't wait. They leave to find cash, and the sale goes with them. For a store owner, a broken or empty ATM during the busiest weeks of the year is a loss you can see walking out the door.

The good news is that this is easy to prevent if you start now. Here's what to check.

## When does the holiday rush start for a store ATM?

Earlier than most people plan for. The National Retail Federation's 2026 forecast covers November 1 through December 31, and it expects retail sales in those two months to grow 4.2% over last year.

That's retail in general, not ATM use. But more shoppers, more events, and more gift buying all mean more people looking for cash. Black Friday is November 27 this year, so you have a few weeks.

## Do people still use cash for holiday shopping?

A lot of them do. Bankrate's 2025 holiday shopping survey found that 49 percent of shoppers planned to pay with cash for some of their winter holiday shopping. Other surveys ask the question in other ways and get different answers, so use that number as a rough guide, not a promise.

The Federal Reserve's 2026 study points the same direction. It found that more than three out of four consumers still carry cash.

Cash also matters for small holiday markets, church bazaars, and craft fairs, where vendors may only take cash. If your store is near one, your ATM may be busier than you expect.

## What should you check before Black Friday?

Start with these four things. Most take ten minutes.

1. **Last December's numbers.** How many withdrawals did your ATM do in the busiest weeks, and when did it run low? Your ATM company should be able to tell you. If it can't, that tells you something too.
2. **The screen and the receipts.** Walk up and use it like a customer. Does it read chip cards? Does it print? Does it show any warning messages?
3. **The connection.** Ask how the machine connects. A machine that loses its connection can't take transactions, no matter how much cash is inside. Ours use a wireless cellular connection, so there's no phone line or shared internet to fail.
4. **The cash level and the plan.** Ask when it was last loaded and when the next load is scheduled.

## How much cash should your ATM hold in December?

There's no honest single number. It depends on your store, your customers, and your machine.

The best guide is your own history. Look at the busiest weeks of last year and plan for more than that, with a cushion. Running out costs you more than having cash left over in January.

Also check the machine's capacity. An ATM holds cash in cassettes. A 1,000-note cassette holds $20,000 in $20 bills, and a 2,000-note cassette holds $40,000. If your machine ran low in the middle of last December, a bigger cassette may help more than loading it more often. We cover the numbers in [the gameday cash planning post](tuscaloosa-gameday-atm-bars.html), and the same math applies here.

## What about security during the holidays?

More cash in the machine means more to protect. Keep the ATM where staff and cameras can see it, and don't leave it unlit or tucked in a back corner. It's also smart to look over the card slot every day for anything that looks added or loose. Our guide to [checking an ATM for a skimmer](how-to-check-atm-for-skimmer.html) walks through it.

## The bottom line

The holiday season is when your store has the most customers and the least patience for a broken ATM. Check last year's numbers, make sure someone is watching the cash level, and fix problems while it's quiet.

You have a few weeks. Use them.
