Every bar owner in Tuscaloosa knows the feeling. It's a home game Saturday. The line is out the door by 10 a.m. The Strip is packed. It's the best day of the month, maybe of the season.

And at 2 p.m., the ATM says "Out of Cash."

From that moment on, every customer who needs cash for a cover, a round, or a tip has to leave to find it. Some come back. A lot of them don't. On a normal Tuesday, that's annoying. On a home game Saturday, it's expensive.

Here's how to make sure your ATM makes it all the way to last call.

Why is gameday so hard on a bar ATM?

Because everything happens at once.

Bryant-Denny Stadium holds more than 100,000 fans. On a home game day, a lot of them, plus the people who never go inside the stadium at all, come through the bars around campus. They come early. They stay late. And many of them need cash.

Cash still matters in a crowd like that. The Federal Reserve's 2026 study found that more than three out of four consumers still carry cash, and more than 90% have no plans to stop using it. In a packed bar, cash is fast. Cover charges, quick rounds, and tips all move quicker with cash than with a card reader and a line.

So an ATM that lasts a normal week can go through several weeks' worth of cash in one Saturday.

How much cash does your ATM need on gameday?

Start with your own numbers. They're better than anyone's guess.

  1. Look at your last big home game. How many withdrawals did your machine do, and when did it run low?
  2. Plan for at least that much, and more for big games: rivalry games, night games, and games with a big visiting crowd.
  3. Add a cushion. Running out at 9 p.m. costs you more than having extra left over.
  4. Load fully the morning of the game. Not Friday afternoon. Not "whenever the route gets there."

If you don't have last season's numbers, your ATM company should. Ask for them.

Is your machine big enough for gameday?

This is the problem a lot of bars don't know they have. Their ATM can't hold enough cash for a big Saturday, no matter how early it's loaded.

An ATM holds cash in cassettes. Here's what common cassette sizes hold in $20 bills:

Cassette size Holds in $20 bills
1,000 notes $20,000
2,000 notes $40,000
Two 2,000-note cassettes $80,000

Many machines can be set up with a larger cassette or a second one. If your bar regularly runs dry on gameday, a bigger setup may fix it without adding a second machine.

Where should your ATM go on gameday?

Placement that works on a Tuesday can cause problems on a Saturday. Think about the line.

  • Keep the line off your bar. A line of people waiting for cash shouldn't block people trying to order.
  • Keep exits and walkways clear. Fire code and crowd flow come first.
  • Keep it where staff and cameras can see it. Crowds are good for business and good cover for trouble.
  • Make it easy to find. If people can't see it, they'll assume you don't have one and leave.

For very busy venues, two machines in two spots can move people faster than one.

What if your current ATM keeps running out on gameday?

That's a sign the machine is too small, the loading plan is wrong, or both.

Start by asking your current provider three questions:

  1. How much did my machine dispense on our last home game day?
  2. When did it run low?
  3. What's your plan for the next one?

The bottom line

Your busiest day shouldn't be the day your ATM gives up. Size the machine for the crowd, load it before the crowd arrives, and put it where the line helps you instead of hurting you.

Home game Saturdays only come a handful of times a season. Make every one of them count.
