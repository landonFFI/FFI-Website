A guest finishes dinner. The check is $48. Four people at the table. One has a card, three want to chip in cash, and nobody has enough.

Now your server is doing math, the guests are digging through pockets, and someone says, "Is there an ATM around here?"

If you've never had to answer that, you may not need one. If you've answered it more than a few times, keep reading. A restaurant is not a bar or a gas station, so the honest answer takes a little more thought.

## Do restaurant guests still pay with cash?

Some do. Fewer than a few years ago, but not none.

The Federal Reserve's 2026 study of how Americans pay found that more than 90% of consumers have no plans to stop using cash. The Fed's earlier work found cash is used most for small purchases, under $25, and shows up more often at places like fast-food restaurants.

That matters for you. A quick-service spot or a counter-service diner sees more cash than a sit-down place with $60 checks. Your own mix of guests is what counts. Our post on [whether cash is really dying](is-cash-dying-2026.html) has the full numbers.

## What can an ATM do for a restaurant?

Three things, in order of how often they matter.

**It catches the guest who comes up short.** Groups that split checks, guests who planned to pay cash, and out-of-towners are the ones who say, "Is there an ATM?" If you don't have one, some leave to find one. Others pay with a card they didn't want to use. A few don't come back.

**It helps with cash tips.** Many servers prefer cash because they are paid on the spot, while card tips can take until the next paycheck, according to a St. Louis Magazine dining column. A guest who wants to tip in cash needs cash in hand.

**It gives you a backup when cards go down.** Card systems fail. In 2021, Chick-fil-A restaurants across the country took only cash for about an hour during a card outage, Fox Business reported. If your card terminal or processor goes down on a Friday night, guests who can pull cash from your ATM can still pay. (A power outage would take down both, so this only helps with card problems.)

It can also earn you a share of the surcharge on each withdrawal, but we'd treat that as the smallest reason, not the biggest.

## What are the downsides for a restaurant?

Let's be straight about these.

- **It uses floor space.** Not much, but in a small dining room every square foot counts.
- **It may not get used much.** One pizzeria owner on a PMQ owner forum said requests came only now and then, and worried about insurance costs. Another owner's math was that they would need around 20 uses a day to cover higher insurance. That was one owner's experience, not a rule, and the numbers were their own.
- **It can look like you want cash only.** Some guests dislike a fee-charging ATM in a dining room. Placement fixes most of that.
- **A bad ATM company is worse than none.** A machine that is always empty or out of order sends guests away annoyed. See [what an "out of order" ATM really costs](atm-out-of-order-cost.html).

## When does a restaurant ATM make sense?

| Your restaurant | ATM likely helps? |
|---|---|
| Counter service or diner with regulars who pay cash | Yes |
| Bar-and-grill with a bar side and cash tips | Yes |
| Spot near a stadium, festival, or busy event area | Yes |
| Large groups and family-style meals | Probably |
| Upscale, card-heavy dining with few cash guests | Probably not |
| An ATM already within a block or two | Probably not |

Here is a simple test. Over the next two weeks, have your staff keep a tally of every time a guest asks about an ATM or pays with cash. If the tally is under a handful, skip it.

## Where should the ATM go in a restaurant?

Out of the way, but easy to find.

- **Near the entrance or waiting area.** Guests who plan ahead will see it.
- **Along the path to the restrooms.** Easy to reach without being the first thing guests see when they walk in.
- **In camera view and staff view.** Good for security.
- **Away from exits and walkways.** Fire code and foot traffic come first.
- **With clear space in front,** so a guest in a wheelchair can use it.
- **Near a standard outlet.** That's the only hookup it needs. Our machines connect by wireless cellular service, so you don't need a phone line or internet.

## The bottom line

An ATM isn't right for every restaurant. It earns its spot when some of your guests pay cash, tip cash, or split checks, and when no other ATM is close by. It's a weak fit for a card-heavy place that rarely sees cash.

Count the cash questions for two weeks. If you hear "Is there an ATM?" more than a few times, it's worth a conversation. If you don't, you've saved yourself the trouble.
